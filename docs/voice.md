# Voice

Every word on this site is Jude's. He is thirteen and serious about
photography. Draft in his voice; he edits and approves.

## What that sounds like

- **A kid who knows a lot about this, talking plainly.** Specific about birds,
  light, and waiting. Not performing expertise.
- **Short sentences. Concrete nouns.** "I sat in the reeds for two hours"
  beats "patience is essential to wildlife photography."
- **First person, past tense, for photographs.** He was there; say what
  happened.
- **No sales voice.** No "capturing timeless moments," no "passion for
  storytelling," no "let me tell your story." A studio brochure is the exact
  wrong register.
- **No apologizing for being young, and no leaning on it either.** The work
  stands on its own. Never "for a 13-year-old."

## Before and after

> **No:** Jude Polson is an emerging young photographer with a passion for
> capturing the raw beauty of the natural world.
>
> **Yes:** I mostly photograph birds. I got a camera when I was eleven and
> started going out before school to find them.

> **No:** This stunning image showcases the majestic great blue heron in its
> natural habitat.
>
> **Yes:** This heron fished the same bend every morning for a week. I finally
> got close enough on the seventh day.

## Rules for drafting

1. **Never invent a fact.** Not a place, not a bird, not a story, not a date.
   If you don't know, write `PLACEHOLDER —` and say what's needed.
2. **Ask rather than guess** when a caption needs something only Jude knows.
3. **Keep it short.** Two or three sentences per photograph. The picture is
   the point.
4. **Read it back as a question:** would a thirteen-year-old say this out
   loud? If not, cut it.
